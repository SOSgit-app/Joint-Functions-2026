Why you are here
You are not here to become an air operations specialist. You are here so that when you return to your squadron — finance, logistics, maintenance, cyber, medical, personnel, intel, acquisitions, security forces, ops, or any other function — you understand the Joint Force problems airpower is asked to solve, the tradeoffs leaders make, and where your work enables or constrains those options.
If you only learn labels, you missed the point. If you can explain the problem, the tradeoff, and what your function must deliver or protect when you get home, you succeeded.

SCENARIO 1 OF 6  ·  CRISIS RESPONSE
Arctic Contingency
The Window Before the Fleet Arrives
 
PURPOSE  This scenario teaches that airpower creates operational options when distance, weather, sea ice, and delayed maritime access constrain the Joint Force. The situation is notional. It is built to mirror publicly documented Arctic operating realities: ice-restricted surface access, weather-killed fixed radars, mobile coastal-defense missiles covering approaches, austere forward detachments, and tanker-dependent defensive persistence.
WHY THE JOINT FORCE IS INVOLVED  A regional competitor is probing a disputed high-latitude archipelago. Seasonal sea ice has closed the direct surface routes. Forward warning sites are already intermittent from ice load and power strain. Maritime forces cannot reposition on the threat’s timeline.
CRISIS TRIGGER  A hostile surface group has closed inside the weapons envelope of key approaches and two forward warning sites. Weather and residual ice make a rapid surface response impossible. The clock is hours, not days.
WHY THIS MATTERS  Lose the approaches and the early-warning picture, and the adversary hardens an anti-access posture before follow-on forces arrive. The commander’s problem is whether the Joint Force still owns the entry.
WHY AIRPOWER MATTERS  Airpower is the only component that can still reach the problem at the speed the ice denies to surface forces. The staff must recommend actions that create time — not actions that wait for the fleet.
Missions in this scenario
1.1  Open the Maritime Approach1.2  Restore the Northern Air Picture1.3  Find the Coastal Missile Threat1.4  Sustain the Forward Interceptor Detachment1.5  Preserve Continuous Defensive Coverage

The Decisive Allocation
 
PURPOSE  This scenario teaches that finite airpower must be prioritized against the Joint Force Commander’s most consequential operational problem. The demand for immediate support across multiple fronts does not automatically justify fragmenting airpower into permanent local allocations. The joint staff must balance urgent tactical requests against threats that could disable theater-wide sustainment and operational reach.
WHY THE JOINT FORCE IS INVOLVED  A multi-axis offensive is underway across an arid theater. Ground formations in contact want persistent air support. Strike capacity cannot cover both.
CRISIS TRIGGER  Troops-in-contact reports are climbing while intelligence confirms launchers preparing against the primary aerial port and logistics hub. The next few hours decide whether the Joint Force still has a theater lifeline.

WHY AIRPOWER MATTERS  Airpower can provide theater fires, defensive counterair, precise CAS, electronic attack, and flexible reallocation. The staff problem is which demand gets finite capacity first.
